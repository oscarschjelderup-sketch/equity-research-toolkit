# 01 – Who is "best"? Sell-side analysts and brokerages covering Oslo Børs

Researcher 1 of 4 · compiled 22 Oct 2026 · English · public sources only

> **Bottom line.** In Norway, *Kapital's annual analyst ranking* (published by Hegnar Media, republished in Finansavisen) is the only public ranking that names individual analysts by sector. In every edition from 2021 to 2025 it ranks **DNB Markets / DNB Carnegie** as the best brokerage. Below that, **ABG Sundal Collier** and **SpareBank 1 Markets (SB1 Markets)** are the clear challengers. The individual winners are very stable, and the same names win the same sector year after year. Among the Nordic-wide surveys, **Kantar Prospera** has ranked DNB first in Norwegian domestic equities for 11 straight years. **Extel** has ranked Carnegie, now DNB Carnegie, first in the Nordics for 10 straight years. These Nordic results are self-reported by the winner, and the full tables are not public.

## 1. Method and sources

**Method.**
- I used web search to find candidate pages, then opened the primary pages directly.
- Kapital and Finansavisen article pages are paywalled. Each one still exposes its headline, its standfirst (`og:description`) and its photo caption in the page head. The 2025 single-category pages also carry an open "Topp tre" table showing vote shares for 2025, 2024 and 2023. I read those fields directly. Nothing in this file comes from paywalled body text.
- Company press pages (DNB, Arctic, Carnegie and Placera/Cision) were read in full. Where a figure comes only from a company's own release, the tables mark it as **self-reported**.
- Where I could see a figure only in a search-engine snippet and the underlying page blocked access, the tables mark it as **snippet-only (unverified)**.
- The web-search budget for this session ran out before every gap could be chased. Section 6 lists what is missing.

## 5. What the rankings measure

| Ranking | Who votes | What is scored | Implication for "best" |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kapital "Norges beste analytikere"** (Hegnar Media; published in Kapital and Finansavisen) | Norway's largest institutional and private investors who actively use sell-side research (S19). The 2025 standfirst describes the voters as investors with more than NOK 1,400bn under management (S15). Brokerages nominate their own analysts and brokers: 2024 had 12 houses, 187 analysts and 134 brokers (S20). Investors name a top 3 in each of **14 categories**, covering sectors plus macro, credit and equity broker (S12). Results are reported as share of votes. 214 analysts and brokers got points in 2024 (S12); 167 analysts and 71 brokers in 2025 (S16). | **Perceived usefulness to large buy-side clients.** It is a popularity and relationship vote, not a measure of forecast accuracy. Search snippets say votes are weighted by investor size, but I could not see the method text, so treat that as unverified. | A large house wins on breadth, as Kapital itself noted (S20, S22). Results are sticky, with the same winners year after year. Kapital found that the 2021 winners' favourite stocks were "banket" by the main index (Kapital, 10 Feb 2022, [link](https://kapital.no/investor/2022/02/10/7812486/norges-beste-analytikere-ble-slatt-kraftig-av-borsen-i-2021)): popularity is not the same as stock-picking alpha. |
| **Kantar (Sifo) Prospera** – Domestic Equity Norway, Nordic Equity | Professional equity investors in Norway and the Nordics, surveyed annually (S21) | Firm-level only, in three subcategories: **Analysis, Execution, Corporate Access** (S21). The DNB release also cites client follow-up, conferences and the quality of analytical conversations (S22). | Measures the whole client service, not individual analysts. No public analyst-level ranking was found. |
| **Extel** (formerly Institutional Investor research rankings) – Nordic and Developed Europe | Investor votes (S26); corporates vote on corporate access | Market share of votes by firm, plus individual analyst and salesperson ranks | Pan-Nordic, so Swedish and Danish coverage carry weight. DNB Carnegie's share fell from nearly 50% (2025) to 40.4% (2026), per its own releases. |
| **StarMine (LSEG, formerly Thomson Reuters/Refinitiv) Nordic awards** | No vote: quantitative | Excess returns of buy/sell calls against an industry benchmark ("Stock Pickers") and EPS-forecast accuracy against peers ("Earnings Estimators") (**snippet-only**) | The only *performance-based* measure. Search snippets mention a 2015 Nordic edition (Handelsbanken top firm, Aukner (DNB) a top individual), but I could not open the release, so this is unverified. No 2016–2026 Nordic edition was found. |

**Takeaway for the toolkit.** "Best" in Norway in practice means *most useful to big Norwegian buy-side clients*. That rewards accessible, consistent, sector-deep analysts at large houses. It does not mean "most accurate". StarMine-style accuracy data for the Nordics is not public after 2015.

## 6. Det som ikke lot seg finne offentlig

- **Full Kapital tables.** These Finansavisen/Kapital articles are paywalled: "Her er alle som fikk poeng i analytikerkåringen 2024" (S12) and "... 2025" (S16), Kapital's "Norges beste analytikere 2022" full list (https://www.kapital.no/investor/2022/10/27/7948094/norges-beste-analytikere-2022), and the bodies of S15 and S17. The overall top-10 lists and the brokerage vote-share tables are therefore not public. The only exceptions are DNB's ">22%" figure for 2024 (search snippet, unverified) and Arctic's 19.6% broker-desk share for 2025.
- **2026 category tables.** The standfirst and photo caption of the 14 Oct 2026 article are public, but the sector-by-sector 2026 winners and vote shares were not found. The paywalled body may say which woman "went to the top"; the open fields do not.
- **Kapital 2015–2020.** No primary source was readable. A search snippet of Kapital's January 2019 issue ("Analytikereliten", https://old.kapital.no/investor/2019/01/analytikereliten-0) names Oddvar Bjørgan (Carnegie) as best overall and DNB Markets as best brokerage. The page failed to load (TLS certificate error), so this is **unverified** and kept out of the tables. The 2011 edition (Realtid, S31) is outside the window: Fridtjof Berents (Arctic) was best overall and Carnegie the best house.
- **Kantar Prospera PDFs** (kantarsifo.se, Norway Domestic Equity 2017–2025). Direct fetches return HTTP 403 and the browser gets a Cloudflare bot check, which I did not try to bypass. Placements for ABG, Pareto, Nordea and SEB come from search snippets only.
- **Extel full Nordic lists.** Extel's results pages need a login. Only winner press releases are public, and the individual ranks, sectors and competitors' shares are not shown. For example, DNB Carnegie names no competitors.
- **StarMine Nordic.** The 2015 Thomson Reuters release redirects to a generic listing. No 2016–2026 Nordic edition was found.
- **LinkedIn posts** (for example SB1 Markets' 2025 summary of its podium places) return HTTP 999 and could not be verified.
- **Firm-level gaps.** No documented ranking placements in 2015–2026 for Danske Bank, Handelsbanken, Nordea (except one snippet), Clarksons Securities, Fearnley Securities, Beringer Finance or Norne.
- **DN and E24 coverage** of the rankings is paywalled or was not found.
- **Search budget.** The session's web-search quota ran out. The 2015–2020 Kapital editions and the 2026 category pages may be findable in a follow-up session.
