## Five findings that shape phases 2-4

1. **"Best" in Norway is a popularity vote, not an accuracy score.** Kapital's ranking is a vote of large
   Norwegian investors (14 categories, vote shares); Prospera scores whole firms on analysis, execution and corporate
   access; Extel is a vote. Kapital itself reported that the 2021 winners' favourite stocks lagged the index. The only
   accuracy-based award (StarMine) has no verifiable Nordic edition after 2015 (01, section 5). The toolkit therefore
   measures itself by calibration (phase 3), not by resemblance to winners.
2. **The structure of a good report is visible; its content mostly is not.** Every public report has a page-one memo
   with the conclusion, the method weights, one quantified value driver and an estimates table; initiations run
   37-66 pages (02, section 3). Full independent broker reports are behind portals, so the sample is commissioned
   and hybrid research.
3. **Three things are missing from every public report in the sample:** a formal reverse DCF (one report backs out
   an implied cost of equity), kill criteria, and a house-vs-consensus table (02, section 3.1). The toolkit has all
   three; they are its differentiators, not imitations.
4. **The most frequent killer on Oslo Børs is leverage and funding** (8 of 21 cases), then growth extrapolated from
   a temporary state, binary events not probability-weighted, and working capital (03, section 2.3). The academic
   evidence agrees on optimism: targets imply about 15pp too much return, and the error grows with the predicted
   upside (03, section 4).
5. **The Norwegian parameters are documented:** PwC/FFN 2025 – market risk premium median 5.0 % (unchanged
   2017-2025), 10-year government bond as rf for 55 % of respondents, size premia by equity value, terminal growth
   and inflation 2.0 %, warning against stacking premia (04, section 2). The binding report-content rules are VPFF
   Standard 3 (February 2026), restating 2016/958 (03 section 3; 04 R37-R40).
